"Scoring" means different things by quiz type: Knowledge Quiz focuses on **right/wrong and totals**, while Scored Quiz focuses on **multi-dimensional analysis and reports**.

## Knowledge Quiz: scoring and judgment

In a **Knowledge Quiz** you can:

- **Set points**: Assign points to questions or correct options.
- **Judge right/wrong**: Mark correct answers; the system judges each question automatically.
- **Sum totals**: Points are added up automatically.
- **Levels**: Split the total into score bands (see [Levels](#levels-and-result-page-actions)) — each band shows its own result instead of a bare number.
- **Answer feedback**: Show respondents the correctness and explanation per question.
- **AI Match**: Grade fill-in-the-blank answers by semantic similarity instead of exact or contains matching, with an accuracy threshold you set. AI grading is unlimited.

<Callout type="info">
  Points and right/wrong judgement are available in Knowledge Quiz and Random Knowledge Quiz. Scored Quiz uses formulas instead, and Outcome Quiz doesn't score at all.
</Callout>

## Outcome: winning by votes

An **Outcome** quiz doesn't score — it **counts votes**. Each option votes for one or more outcome types, and the type with the most votes wins; ties go to whichever comes first in the outcome list.

Every outcome can carry its own CTA button, wiring an entertaining result straight into a next step. See [The four quiz types](/en/getting-started/quiz-types#outcome-quiz).

## Scored Quiz: dimensional analysis

In a **Scored Quiz**, scoring is more about "profiling" than right/wrong:

- **Multi-dimensional scoring**: Define several dimensions, each with **its own formula** over question codes; after submission, each dimension's formula gives its score, often shown as a **radar chart**. A dimension with no formula always scores 0.
- **Result profile**: Produce personality/ability conclusions based on dimension scores.

### Which questions are scored

A Scored Quiz total comes **only from the formula**, which references question codes. Only questions that yield a number can be referenced: single choice, multiple choice, dropdown and true/false (via per-option scores), cascade, rating, number, date and time. A choice question switched to **Collect data only** can't be referenced either.

**Fill-in-the-blank only collects text and is never scored** — free text cannot take part in arithmetic, so it produces no score and does not appear among the insertable fields in the formula editor. That makes it the right way to ask one open-ended question ("anything else we should know?") inside a scored assessment.

<Callout type="warning">
  With no formula the total score is empty, no level can ever match, and the overall-assessment block disappears from the result page. Whenever you configure levels, configure the formula too.
</Callout>

## Levels and result-page actions

Split the total score into **levels** (0–60 needs work / 60–80 good / 80+ excellent). Each carries a name, colour and description, and can hold a **CTA button** — the low band might offer "Book a consultation". This is the step that wires an assessment into conversion.

<Callout type="info">
  Don't want respondents seeing a number? You can **hide the overall score** and keep only the level name, description and summary — better for quizzes that should feel like a diagnosis rather than an exam.
</Callout>

## Report generation

- **Synchronous**: Basic scoring is calculated immediately after submission.
- **Asynchronous**: Heavier computation such as dimensional analysis is generated asynchronously; reports go through a "generating → completed" state, and the system retries automatically on failure.

For viewing, exporting, and delivering reports, see [Reports](/en/results/reports).
