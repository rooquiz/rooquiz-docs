The public link is the most common way to distribute a quiz. Anyone with the link can open the answer page and fill it in (whether sign-in is required depends on the quiz settings).

## Get and share the link

<Steps>
### Open the share page

In the target quiz, open **Share** → **Share Link**.

### Copy the public link

The system generates a unique public link for each quiz, shown under **Link Address**. Click **Copy** and distribute it via chat, email, communities, and other channels, or **Open** it to check what respondents see.

### Set a custom path (optional)

Under **Custom path**, give the quiz a memorable address such as `burnout-test` and click **Save**. Leave it empty to keep the random one.

<Callout type="warning">
  Changing the custom path **breaks the old address right away** — links, QR codes and posts that used it stop working. Set it before you distribute, and avoid changing it afterwards.
</Callout>

### Generate a QR code

The same link can produce a **QR code**, handy for posters, offline scenarios, or mobile scan-to-answer. Click **Download QR Code** to save the image.
</Steps>

## Characteristics of the link

- **Unique**: Each quiz has its own public link.
- **Whether sign-in is needed**: decided by [Submission Access](/en/editor/settings#submission-access) — the default **Open to everyone** needs none; **Email required to see the report** lets anyone answer but asks for an email before the report (the **Verify the email before showing the report** switch, off by default, adds a 6-digit code); **Sign in to answer** requires signing in before answering.
- **Your own domain**: on the Pro plan, links can use your team's [custom domain](/en/teams/custom-domain) instead of the platform one.
- **Trackable source**: Combine with [UTM Source Tracking](/en/publish/tracking) to distinguish responses by channel.
- **Multilingual**: a quiz with [language versions](/en/editor/translations) keeps one link; each language opens with `?lang=xx` added to it (e.g. `?lang=fr`). **Settings → Language** lists the versions with a button to open each one.

QR codes suit posters, printed material, chat apps and slide decks — anywhere scanning is faster than typing. The image is downloadable.
